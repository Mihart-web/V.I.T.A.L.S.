Extreme cold, altitude and polar isolation

A high-altitude Antarctic research scenario modeled on Amundsen–Scott South Pole Station. The values below describe that reference station, not all of Antarctica.

This is a scenario-level reference. No local observation, health diagnosis, alert, or forecast is generated.

OPERATIONS / SCIENTIFIC INTERPRETATION

Risk & Crew Considerations

01 / ANNUAL MEAN AIR TEMPERATURE

−49 °C

Approximate station climatology. Severe cold requires layered protection, thermal management and shelter access.

02 / ELEVATION

2,835 m

Station elevation above sea level. Station elevation creates reduced oxygen partial pressure compared with sea level.

03 / AIR PRESSURE

Reduced

High-altitude; measure locally. Polar day and polar night can challenge circadian rhythms and sleep.

04 / DAYLIGHT

Polar day / night

Season-dependent extended cycles. Weather and seasonal access can constrain emergency evacuation.

05 / AIR HUMIDITY

Very low

Polar desert conditions. Severe cold requires layered protection, thermal management and shelter access.

06 / OPERATIONAL HAZARD

Cold exposure

Isolation and limited winter access. Station elevation creates reduced oxygen partial pressure compared with sea level.
