PLANETARY OVERVIEW

Exploring the Lunar Environment

Earth's Moon is a rocky world with one-sixth Earth's gravity, a nearly airless surface and large temperature swings between sunlight and darkness. Galactic cosmic rays, solar energetic particles and abrasive regolith are hazards for surface operations. Values below describe reference conditions, not a specific landing site or crew exposure.

RADIATION ENVIRONMENT

Lunar Radiation Environment

The Moon lacks substantial atmospheric shielding and a global magnetic field. Galactic cosmic rays and solar energetic particles are relevant hazards for lunar surface operations.

Exposure depends on location, mission duration, solar activity, shielding and operational conditions. No astronaut-specific absorbed or effective dose is assigned.

LUNAR ENVIRONMENT / REGOLITH

Lunar Regolith

Lunar regolith consists of mineral particles, impact glass and fragmented rock. Fine particles can adhere to spacesuits and equipment; abrasive dust may affect seals, mechanical components and optical surfaces.

Dust carried into habitats can present potential respiratory, ocular and skin exposure concerns. This page does not estimate astronaut-specific concentrations or clinical risk.
